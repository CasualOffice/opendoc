# 179 — GPU render backend, measured: the time goes to glyph rasterization, and the sparse-strip pipeline wins most of it, on either processor

**Status:** Measured and built 2026-10-24. Phase 1 (measure and decide) and Phase 2 (an opt-in
backend with a parity gate and a benchmark) are both in this change.
**Decides:** ADR-074 (proposed). **Row:** `docs/109` RM-14.
**Builds on:** `56` §5 (the backend-neutral display list, "later — GPU (WebGPU)"), `43` §5.2
(pluggable backends), `55` (rendering fidelity), `107` §4 (editing stays light).

---

## 1. The question, and the short answer

RM-14 said that "the GPU half of the render backend has never been begun". Before choosing a
GPU stack, this document measures where today's CPU raster spends its time. It then measures
four ways of spending less.

- **Where the time goes:** glyph rasterization. On every page measured it is 85–99 % of
  `render`. Outline extraction (`skrifa`) is under 5 %. Vector furniture, the surface
  allocation and the RGBA copy together are a few percent. Pictures are the exception: a
  picture costs in proportion to its device area (§3).
- **What fixes it:** the sparse-strip rasterizer (`vello_common`), not the GPU as such. It is
  fed by one new encoder from our display list. On a dense page at 100 % it costs **6.5 ms on
  the CPU (`vello_cpu`) and 7.6 ms on the GPU (`vello_hybrid`), against 26.0 ms for
  `tiny-skia`**. At 200 % on a DPR-2 screen the figures are 15.9 ms, 16.9 ms and 75.9 ms (§4).
  The GPU removes only the fine-rasterization stage, and on an Apple M4 that buys about as
  much as it costs to submit and synchronise.
- **In WebAssembly** the same encoder on `vello_cpu` is **2.1x faster than `tiny-skia` as the
  editor ships today (scalar)**, and **3.6–4.6x faster with `simd128`** (§4.2).
- **Recommendation (ADR-074):** adopt the `vello_common` encoder as the second backend, with
  `tiny-skia` kept as the golden reference. Make **`vello_cpu` (with `simd128`) the candidate
  for the next default in the browser**, behind its own A/B on the shipped editor. Keep
  **`vello_hybrid` (WebGPU, then WebGL2) opt-in**, for very large pixel counts and for hosts
  that own a GPU surface. Do not adopt `vello` (the compute-shader renderer), a hand-written
  `wgpu` renderer, or a page bitmap cache as *the* fix (§7).

## 2. Method and instruments

Every number below comes from a committed instrument, and its output is committed beside it
under `benchmarks/reports/179/`.

| Instrument | What it measures | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |

| `crates/casual-doc-render-gpu/examples/gpu_profile.rs` | `tiny-skia` vs `vello_cpu` vs `vello_hybrid` (submit only, to a texture, with readback), plus difference from the reference | `backend-comparison.csv` |
| `benchmarks/render-wasm/run.sh` (outside the workspace) | `tiny-skia` vs `vello_cpu` inside wasm, in scalar and `simd128` builds, and module sizes | `wasm.csv` |
| `benchmarks/render-wasm/blit.mjs` | `putImageData` cost in Chromium; WebGPU/WebGL2 availability | `blit.json` |

- **Documents.** The repository's `sample.docx` (14 pages, one with a picture) and a
  deterministic **heavy** document generated by
  `crates/casual-doc-render/examples/support/heavy_docx.rs`. Its first page is dense 9 pt
  justified text with mixed bold, italic, underline and highlight, a shaded 6 × 12 table and a
  line of emoji. It is 6,783–7,309 glyphs a page against `sample.docx`'s 380–2,268.
- **Scales.** 96 dpi (100 % at DPR 1), 192 dpi (200 % at DPR 1, or 100 % at DPR 2) and
  384 dpi (200 % at DPR 2). The editor rasterizes at `96 · zoom · min(DPR, 2)`
  (`webapp/src/main.js` `BASE_DPI`, `MAX_BACKING_DPR`), so 384 dpi is a Retina laptop at
  200 %.
- **Fonts.** Bundled faces only (`ParleyShaper::without_system_fonts`), which is what a browser
  has, so native and wasm do the same work.
- **Machine.** Apple M4, 10 cores, macOS. Medians of 7 runs after a warm-up, on a machine
  shared with other build lanes. The ratios are stable from run to run; single cells can
  move by 10–20 % (one `heavy` p2 `simd` row in `wasm.csv` is an outlier of this kind and is
  not quoted).
- **wasm.** Built for `wasm32-unknown-unknown` at `opt-level = 3` (the level `webapp/build.sh`
  keeps for the layout and raster crates) and run in Node 24 (V8, the engine Chromium uses).

## 3. Where the CPU raster's time goes

From `cpu-raster-profile.csv`. Each `render_ms` is the whole page; the family columns repeat
the render with the display list filtered to that family (clip and layer brackets kept).

| Page | Glyphs | dpi | render | glyphs only | vector only | pictures only | outline extraction | alloc | copy out |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| heavy p2 | 7,309 | 96 | 25.52 | 25.32 | 0.07 | — | 1.17 | 0.08 | 0.05 |
| heavy p2 | 7,309 | 384 | 73.32 | 72.15 | 0.10 | — | 1.17 | 1.24 | 1.18 |
| sample p12 | 2,268 | 96 | 6.93 | 6.88 | — | — | 0.36 | 0.08 | 0.05 |
| sample p12 | 2,268 | 384 | 19.98 | 19.69 | — | — | 0.36 | 1.22 | 1.20 |
| sample p1 (picture) | 405 | 96 | 3.86 | 1.66 | — | 1.59 | 0.06 | 0.08 | 0.05 |
| sample p1 (picture) | 405 | 384 | 17.92 | 4.68 | — | 13.33 | 0.06 | 1.23 | 1.30 |

All times are in ms. A "—" is a family with nothing on that page; its measured value is under
0.1 ms or is negative noise.

What it says:

1. **Text is the cost.** It is linear in glyphs and grows sub-linearly in pixels: 4x the
   pixels costs about 2.9x. `tiny-skia` fills each run's outlines as one anti-aliased path,
   and its per-glyph edge setup dominates.
2. **Finding the glyph shape is not the problem.** `skrifa` outline extraction is about
   0.16 µs a glyph, 1–5 % of the page, and independent of scale. A glyph *outline* cache would
   buy almost nothing. A glyph *coverage* cache (an atlas) would, and §7 explains why it is
   not the recommended fix.
3. **Pictures scale with area.** The page with one picture spends 74 % of its 384 dpi time
   decoding and bilinear-scaling it, every time the page is painted, because the CPU raster
   decodes media at paint (`render_image`).
4. **Zoom and scroll cost a full re-raster.** A zoom change re-renders every visible page at
   the new dpi. Scrolling a page back into the window re-renders it, because
   `webapp/src/editor_paint.mjs` drops a page's canvas when it leaves the window
   (`dematerializePage` → `releasePageCanvas`) and `paintPageCanvas` rebuilds it with
   `renderPage`. Both therefore cost the `render` column above, plus the hand-off of §4.3.
   Editing re-renders the edited page the same way.

## 4. The candidates, measured

### 4.1 Native (Apple M4, Metal)

From `backend-comparison.csv`. `cpu` is the shipped path (allocate, `render`, copy out).
`gpu view` renders into a texture the host owns and waits for the GPU, which is what a host
with a WebGPU canvas pays. `gpu rgba` adds the readback.

| Page | dpi | `tiny-skia` | `vello_cpu` | `vello_hybrid` submit | `vello_hybrid` view | `vello_hybrid` rgba |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| heavy p2 | 96 | 26.04 | 6.51 | 6.29 | 7.59 | 7.64 |
| heavy p2 | 192 | 45.18 | 9.36 | 7.52 | 10.51 | 11.83 |
| heavy p2 | 384 | 75.86 | 15.89 | 10.54 | 16.87 | 16.71 |
| sample p12 | 96 | 7.14 | 2.04 | 1.90 | 3.21 | 3.27 |
| sample p12 | 384 | 22.95 | 7.62 | 3.08 | 8.25 | 7.16 |
| sample p8 (light) | 96 | 2.19 | 0.61 | 0.52 | 1.80 | 1.94 |
| sample p8 (light) | 384 | 8.30 | 5.39 | 0.94 | 4.77 | 4.90 |
| sample p7 (picture) | 384 | 19.50 | 17.56 | 13.07 | 15.72 | 19.57 |

All times are in ms.

- On dense text the sparse-strip pipeline is **4–5x faster than `tiny-skia`, on either
  processor**.
- `vello_hybrid`'s **submit** column is the CPU work of a GPU frame: path flattening and strip
  building. It is most of the frame. The GPU's fine stage saves the rest, but a submit and
  wait costs about 1.2 ms on Metal, so on a light page at 96 dpi the GPU is *slower* than
  `vello_cpu` (1.80 vs 0.61 ms). At 384 dpi on light pages it is ahead (4.77 vs 5.39 ms).
- **Pictures do not get faster.** They take the reference renderer's fallback (§6), which
  decodes and scales on the CPU exactly as today. Native image handling (decode once, upload
  once, sample on the GPU) is the follow-up that fixes pictures for every backend (§9).

### 4.2 WebAssembly (V8)

From `wasm.csv`. `scalar` is how `webapp/build.sh` builds today (no `simd128`).

| Page | dpi | `tiny-skia` scalar | `vello_cpu` scalar | `tiny-skia` simd128 | `vello_cpu` simd128 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| heavy p1 | 96 | 44.15 | 21.26 | 47.56 | 11.41 |
| heavy p1 | 384 | 123.66 | 60.40 | 119.07 | 32.86 |
| heavy p3 | 96 | 31.69 | 14.25 | 30.34 | 7.06 |
| heavy p3 | 384 | 88.89 | 41.27 | 74.29 | 16.20 |
| sample p12 | 96 | 13.84 | 6.80 | 11.93 | 3.12 |
| sample p12 | 384 | 40.72 | 21.92 | 33.89 | 9.51 |
| sample p1 (picture) | 384 | 78.72 | 86.05 | 31.50 | 30.07 |

All times are in ms.

- The editor's own raster in the browser is **about 1.8x slower than native** on dense text
  (44 vs 24 ms for heavy p1 at 100 %). A dense page at 200 % on a Retina screen takes
  **124 ms**: one dropped frame per page that scrolls in, and one per keystroke on that page.
- `vello_cpu` halves that in the shipped (scalar) configuration, and with `simd128` it reaches
  **3.6–4.6x**: 32.9 ms instead of 123.7 ms. `vello_cpu` gains far more from `simd128` than
  `tiny-skia` does, because its fine stage is written over `fearless_simd`.
- `simd128` is supported by every browser the editor targets (Chrome 91, Firefox 89,
  Safari 16.4). Turning it on is a build flag. It is not taken here: it is a change to
  `webapp/build.sh` and to the shipped module, and it is RM-14's next increment (§9).
- The scalar picture page is slower on `vello_cpu` (86 vs 79 ms), because the fallback tile is
  drawn by `tiny-skia` and then composited by a scalar fine stage. It is one more argument for
  native pictures (§9).

### 4.3 Handing the page to the browser

From `blit.json` (headless Chromium 156): `new ImageData` + `putImageData` costs **1.3 ms at
96 dpi, 3.0 ms at 192 dpi and 7.6 ms at 384 dpi**. The wasm `render_page` copy out
(`surface.data().to_vec()`) adds another 0.05–1.2 ms (§3). A GPU host that renders straight
into a WebGPU canvas skips both. That is the GPU path's real advantage in a browser, and at
384 dpi it is worth about 9 ms a page.

Headless Chromium on this machine reports **no WebGPU adapter and WebGL2 present**. That is
the case the WebGL2 fallback exists for, and it is why a WebGPU-only renderer is ruled out
(§7).

From `wasm.csv`. The bench module is built at `opt-level = 3` and passed through
`wasm-opt -Oz`, so its absolute size is not the shipped editor's. The **deltas** are the
point:

| Build | `wasm-opt -Oz` bytes | Δ | gzip -9 bytes | Δ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| base (`tiny-skia` only) | 19,907,098 | — | 10,196,218 | — |

`vello_cpu` costs +2.6 % (+1.6 % gzipped). The GPU stack costs +6.8 % (+3.7 % gzipped), plus

## 6. Determinism and parity: the CPU stays the reference

`tiny-skia` (`casual-doc-render`) stays the default backend and the golden reference. No
golden moves. The second backend is held to it **within a stated tolerance**, never
pixel-identically, because two anti-aliasers legitimately disagree by a few levels along every
edge. `vello_cpu` and `vello_hybrid` build the same strips from the same commands, so they
agree with each other almost exactly: their difference from the reference matches to 0.002
mean on every page in `backend-comparison.csv`.

**The tolerance** (`crates/casual-doc-render-gpu/tests/support/mod.rs`), per page:

| Measure | Limit | Worst seen |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| mean channel difference (0–255, all channels) | 3.0 | 2.018 (heavy p1, 96 dpi) |
| share of pixels whose worst channel differs by more than 64 | 0.1 % | 0.0499 % (feature sheet, 96 dpi) |
| such pixels in the worst 32 × 32 square | 48 | 38 (feature sheet, 192 dpi) |

The third measure exists because the first two can miss one missing word on a full page. Edge
disagreement is scattered; a missing object is concentrated.

**Two tests, both painters:**

- `tests/parity.rs`: every page of `sample.docx`, the heavy document, and 11 generated
  fixtures (shapes, preset and custom geometry, VML, a chart, the rotated multiply-blended
  watermark, emoji in every container, wrapped pictures, merged tables, mixed scripts,
  grouped text boxes), at 96 and 192 dpi.
- `tests/feature_sheet.rs`: one synthetic page that puts every family where a mistake shows.
  It has a fill larger than its clip, nested clips, a multiply layer over colour, a rotated
  layer with text, linear and radial gradients, a dashed curved outline, a rotated flipped
  shape, all five arrowheads, flat furniture, a sub-pixel rule, and one real run in every
  underline style, both strikes, every text effect and a 150 % scale. Real documents leave
  two of these unexercised: no fixture inks outside a clip, and the watermark multiplies over
  plain paper, where multiply and normal give the same pixels.

The `vello_cpu` tests run everywhere, including CI's Linux runners. The `vello_hybrid` tests
run where an adapter exists (CI's macOS runner, a developer's machine), and elsewhere they say
`SKIPPED: no GPU adapter` and pass. A missing GPU is the documented "use the CPU raster" case.

**Each guard was seen to fail** (`SKILL` §4). Each defect below was put back into the encoder
and the suite run:

| Mutation | Caught by |
| --- | --- |
| glyphs one pixel low | both tests; sheet worst tile 300 |
| no hairline snap (sub-pixel borders vanish) | sheet, 0.128 % disagreeing; heavy p1 0.73 % |
| clips ignored | sheet: mean 8.8, worst tile 1,024 |
| single underline dropped | sheet worst tile 65; heavy p2 1.15 % |
| fallback tiles dropped (pictures, arrowheads) | both tests: worst tile 93, `sample` p1 0.70 % |
| multiply layer drawn normal | sheet worst tile 1,024 |
| scattered words dropped | parity: `sample` p2 worst tile 96 at 0.037 % disagreeing, which the page-wide share alone would have passed |

## 7. Candidates weighed

| Candidate | Throughput | Browser reach | Determinism | Size | Text, COLR | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **`vello_cpu`** (sparse strips, CPU) | 4–5x native, 2.1x / 3.6–4.6x wasm (§4) | every browser; no GPU needed | integer `u8` pipeline, same input → same pixels on one SIMD level; tolerance-checked against the reference | +0.52 MB | our outlines, exactly as the reference; colour glyphs via the reference's tile | **next default candidate (browser), behind an A/B** |
| **`vello_hybrid`** (sparse strips, GPU fine stage) | ≈ `vello_cpu` on an M4; ahead at high pixel counts; skips `putImageData` (≈ 9 ms at 384 dpi) | WebGPU now, WebGL2 through `vello_hybrid/webgl`; native Metal, Vulkan, DX12 | GPU varies by vendor; tolerance-checked, never golden | +1.35 MB plus glue | as `vello_cpu` | **opt-in** |
| `vello` (compute shaders) | not measured | **WebGPU only: no WebGL2 path**; 0.11 needs Rust 1.89, above the 1.88 MSRV | as above | larger | its own glyph pipeline | rejected: no fallback where WebGPU is absent (§4.3) |
| hand-written `wgpu` renderer (tessellation plus a glyph atlas) | unknown | as `vello_hybrid` | own AA to validate | small | a second text renderer to write | rejected: rebuilds what `vello_common` already is |
| page bitmap / tile cache over `tiny-skia` | removes *re-showing* a page that is unchanged | everywhere | exact | none | exact | not *the* fix: it does nothing for zoom or edits, and a cached page is 55 MB at 384 dpi. Worth having later on top of any backend (§9) |
| glyph coverage atlas in `tiny-skia` | likely large on text | everywhere | sub-pixel positions are quantised, so pixels move from today's goldens | none | per glyph | not chosen: it changes the reference's own pixels and adds a cache the sparse pipeline makes unnecessary |

## 8. What is built

**`crates/casual-doc-render-gpu`** (workspace member; nothing depends on it by default):

- `encode` — one pass over the `DisplayList` onto a `SparseCanvas` (the `vello_common`
  drawing API that `vello_hybrid::Scene` and `vello_cpu::RenderContext` share). Its
  complexity is O(items + glyphs) per page. Geometry rules are copied from the reference:
  the hairline snap, unhinted outlines at the run's device size, the face's own underline and
  strike metrics, the size-derived effect offsets, quadratic rounded corners, preset dash
  ratios, the gradient extent and the `a:xfrm` transform.
- `tile` — the **fallback**. Colour-glyph runs, shapes with arrowheads, and every picture are
  painted by `casual-doc-render` itself into a tile covering the item's box and composited as
  an image. The item is translated on a twip that is also a whole device pixel, so at every
  multiple of 96 dpi (and at 144 dpi) the tile lands texel for texel. That keeps a single
  implementation of each rare family, and it makes them the reference's pixels.
- `gpu` (feature `gpu`) — `GpuRenderer`. It requests a headless adapter or wraps a host's
  device. `render_to_view` draws into a host texture with no readback; `render_rgba` returns
  premultiplied RGBA with `Surface::data`'s contract. Every error is "use the CPU raster for
  this page": no adapter, no device, or a page larger than the device's texture limit or than
  65,535 px.
- `cpu` (feature `sparse-cpu`) — `render_sparse_cpu`, the same encoder on `vello_cpu`.

**Paint-family support**, by family rather than by success (`SKILL` §9.3):

| Family | How the second backend paints it |
| --- | --- |
| outline glyph runs, horizontal scale | native (vector) |
| underline: single, double, thick, dotted, dashed, dot-dash, words, wavy; strike; double strike | native |
| text effects: shadow, emboss, imprint, outline (on outline glyphs) | native |
| colour glyphs (COLR v0/v1, CBDT, sbix), and their effects | fallback tile (reference pixels) |
| rect, ellipse, rounded rect, polygon, line | native |
| DrawingML shape: solid and linear/radial gradient fill, dashed outline, rotation and flip | native |
| DrawingML shape with arrowheads | fallback tile |
| pictures (raster and, on native, SVG; crop, opacity, rotation) | fallback tile |
| clip, nested clip | native (clip layers) |
| layer: rotation and flip, multiply blend | native (transformed vectors and a blend layer). The reference composites an upright offscreen bitmap through the transform with bilinear sampling, so the GPU's rotated text is sharper; this is inside the tolerance |

**Opt-ins:**

- Native hosts: `casual_doc_render_gpu::gpu::GpuRenderer` and `cpu::render_sparse_cpu`.
- The editor's wasm: `casual-doc-wasm` feature `sparse-raster` adds `renderPageSparse(index,
  dpi)`, with `renderPage`'s exact contract (`crates/casual-doc-wasm/src/sparse_raster.rs`).
  It is not enabled by `webapp/build.sh`, and nothing in `webapp/src` calls it, so the
  shipped module and the editor's default path are unchanged.
- Benchmark: `cargo run --release -p casual-doc-render-gpu --all-features --example
  gpu_profile`.

## 9. Not built, in order

1. **Build the shipped module with `simd128`** and measure `tiny-skia` in it. It is a
   `webapp/build.sh` change, and it carries its own size and wasm-size-test update.
2. **A/B `renderPageSparse` against `renderPage` in the shipped editor**, measuring time to
   interactive and per-keystroke repaint, with `simd128`. Only after that, propose
   `vello_cpu` as the browser default (`docs/107` §4: per-interaction work stays light).
3. **Native pictures:** decode once per media id, keep the pixmap (CPU) or texture (GPU), and
   sample it, instead of decoding at every paint. This helps every backend, the reference
   included.
4. **The browser GPU path:** an async `WasmGpuRenderer` over a WebGPU `OffscreenCanvas`, and
   the `vello_hybrid/webgl` painter for WebGL2, both rendering straight into the canvas with
   no `putImageData`.
5. **Keep a page's raster across a scroll-out** (a bounded cache keyed by page, revision and
   dpi), so scrolling back costs nothing on any backend.
6. Native colour glyphs and arrowheads in the encoder, if a profile ever shows the fallback
   tiles mattering. Today they are rare and exact.

## 10. Open questions

- **Q1.** Is `vello_cpu` (pre-1.0, Linebender) an acceptable *default* dependency for the
  browser, given that the reference stays `tiny-skia`? Its licence (Apache-2.0 OR MIT) and
  MSRV (0.2 needs 1.88) fit.
- **Q2.** Once item 4 of §9 lands, should the GPU path be offered to hosts at all by default,
  or only on request? On an M4 it is not faster than `vello_cpu`; on weak mobile CPUs it may
  be. That needs a measurement on a phone.
- **Q3.** Should `simd128` become a hard requirement of the editor? It would exclude Safari
  before 16.4.
